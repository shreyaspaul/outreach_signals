# Generate Outreach Messages (Claude-written)

You write the messages yourself, in this session. No LLM API is called for the
writing. A helper script handles data in/out so your only job is the thinking
and the writing.

## Procedure

1. **Dump the prospect bundles** (deterministic, no API):
   ```bash
   python scripts/prep_bundles.py dump <enriched.csv> --limit <N> -o data/message_bundles.json
   ```
   Default input is the latest `data/enriched_*.csv`; default N is all gradeable rows.

2. **Read** `data/message_bundles.json`. It has `data_dictionary` (what every field
   means) and `prospects` (each with `domain`, `name`, and a full `bundle` audit).

3. **For each prospect**, do the two passes in your head (Analyst, then Writer)
   per the spec below, and produce one result object.

4. **Write all results** to `data/message_results.json` as a JSON array (schema at
   the bottom). One object per prospect, keyed by its `domain`.

5. **Assemble the CSV**:
   ```bash
   python scripts/prep_bundles.py assemble <enriched.csv> data/message_results.json -o <out.csv>
   ```
   This runs `sanitize()` (em-dash strip, `{first-name}` enforcement) for you.

6. **Report**: show the user a few first_messages and flag anything weak.

## THE PHILOSOPHY (read this twice)

The best cold outreach does not flatter. It **demonstrates understanding**. The
prospect should feel *seen*, like you actually get how their business works, not
buttered up. Understanding outranks praise every single time.

We are sitting on something most cold emailers never have: a deep, real audit of
each prospect's site (traffic, stack, funding, real-user speed, design, content,
accessibility, the facts they're proud of). Use it to say something **true and
specific about their situation that they'll quietly agree with.** That agreement
is what earns the rest of the message.

We sell WordPress -> Webflow migrations / site rebuilds to funded SaaS & tech
startups. The through-line of every message: *their traction is real, and the
site is the one piece with room to catch up, and at their scale that's worth real
money.* Never negative, never fear, always ends forward.

## MESSAGE ANATOMY (4 short beats, one idea per line)

1. **Open with a light, human cue** — how you came across them or a quick frame.
   Plain and real ("hey {first-name}, came across [company] looking at [category]
   tools"). No "hope you're well", no compliment-gushing.

2. **THE INFERENCE (this is the whole email).** State one *true, specific
   inference about how their business or site actually works*, drawn from the
   data, as a humble hypothesis. This replaces flattery. It must make them think
   "huh, yeah, that's right." See the deep dive below.

3. **The grounded observation + concrete outcome.** Name the ONE real thing from
   the audit (kindly, factually, never judged) and tie it straight to a concrete
   business result. When traffic is high, do the math out loud: at their scale,
   even a small lift in conversion is a large, tangible number of signups/revenue.

4. **A direct, signal-matched CTA (get this exactly right).** ONE short,
   low-pressure question **about the SAME thing you just pitched** — intentional and
   specific, not a vague chat-opener. Match it to the chosen signal:
   - performance / speed → "is site performance something you're looking at?" /
     "is speed on your radar at all?"
   - design / distinctiveness → "is a refresh something you're considering?" /
     "is a redesign on your radar?"
   - content / depth / clarity → "is building out the site something you're
     thinking about?"
   A soft yes/no question like these is CORRECT and wanted. **BANNED: vague,
   open-ended conversation-starters** that don't name the pitch — "how are you
   thinking about the site?", "what do you most want visitors to take away?",
   "curious how you're approaching X", "how are you thinking about that first
   impression?". Never a calendar link, never "want the report?". The whole message
   is intentional; the CTA must point straight at the one thing you raised.

Optional: a genuine, understated nod to a real strength can live inside beat 2 or
3 — but as *recognition*, not gushing ("the names you've got behind you" not
"your AMAZING world-class brand").

## THE INFERENCE MOVE (deep dive — get this right)

A great inference connects two data points into a read on their *business*, then
states it humbly so it invites a reply. Hedge it: "i'd guess", "usually means",
"if you're like most". A confident guess that's 80% right beats a safe platitude.

Patterns that work, grounded in our fields:

- **High traffic + strong proof -> warm-led growth.**
  "with the traffic you're pulling and names like {proof} behind you, i'd guess
  most of your signups already arrive sold, word of mouth, your community, repeat
  users. which means the site's real job is winning the people landing cold."
  (Mirrors the great email's "no big sales team -> growth is referrals" move.)

- **Funded + old/WordPress stack -> the site was 'good enough' and is now the drag.**
  "raising {funding} and still on wordpress usually means the site got you here
  fine, but it's starting to hold back what your team can ship more than it shows."

- **Design-led / visual product + design has headroom -> the cold visitor gap.**
  "for a product this visual, the site is the first taste people get. i'd guess it
  converts the folks who already trust you fine, the gap is the ones meeting you
  cold."

- **Big audience + a real on-site friction (speed / a11y / clarity) -> leak at scale.**
  "at the traffic you're doing, even a small share of people bouncing on a slow
  first load adds up to a lot of lost signups a month."

Rules for the inference:
- It must be **derivable from the actual data** in the bundle. Never invent facts,
  numbers, funding, or customers. Use exact numbers/names only as given.
- It must be **flattering by implication** (it credits their traction/judgment)
  while pointing at the opportunity. Understanding, never a gotcha.
- Keep it to one or two lines. Don't over-explain it.
- **NEVER sound arrogant, presumptuous, or superior.** Do NOT frame their visitors
  as skeptics, judges, or evaluators scrutinizing them, and do NOT imply the site
  fails to live up to what they've built. **Banned framings:** "evaluating you
  against your own promise", "judging you in seconds", "sizing you up", "before
  they read a word", "deciding whether to trust you", "proving you're as fast as
  you claim". These read as snide and condescending — a real person would be
  offended, not intrigued. Instead credit how they genuinely grow (warm audience,
  word of mouth, reputation) and frame the site's job as a friendly, forward
  opportunity, never as the reader having to rescue a site that's falling short.

## CHOOSING THE PRIMARY SIGNAL (the ONE thing you name in message 1)

Most bundles have several usable signals. Pick ONE for message 1 (never club unrelated
problems into a list — that reads like an unsolicited audit and kills the tone). The
second signal is already staged for message 2 (`secondary_point`), so you are not leaving
it on the table; you are pacing it.

**Rank candidates by likely impact on THIS business's conversion/growth — NOT by how
"objective" or measurable the signal is.** Objectivity is about *defensibility* (can we
state it as a hard fact), not importance. In order:

1. **Lead with whatever most plausibly moves their numbers, given how they grow (the
   inference).** A mediocre/generic **design is a first-class conversion lever** when the
   site's real job is winning cold visitors, especially for premium, consumer, visual, or
   trust-driven products, and it can and should outrank a technically-measurable but
   low-impact defect. Do NOT default to "design" just because it scored low, though: name
   it only when the design is genuinely the thing costing conversions.
2. **Performance leads** when slow load or layout shift is plausibly costing conversions
   (a slow first load before anyone sees value; a page that jumps around while deciding).
   **AI-search-invisibility (`ai_invisible_client_rendered` / `blocks_ai_crawlers`) can be
   PROMOTED to the message-1 lead** — not just the msg-2 secondary — when the business is
   discovery / content / SEO-driven and being unfindable in AI answers is the real growth
   threat. Only when it is actually detected in the bundle.
3. **Concreteness is a tie-breaker, not the ranking key.** When two candidates look
   similarly impactful, prefer the one you can cite as a hard fact.
4. **When nothing dominates** (the "solid but plays it safe everywhere" middling sites),
   lead with the business-inference THEME ("the site's solid but plays it safe, room to
   feel as distinctive as the product") rather than forcing a weak "design" vs "content"
   label.
5. **Down-weight low-probability / low-severity signals.** Accessibility rarely leads —
   most sites never face a lawsuit, it's a "might", and legal-threat framing breaks the
   no-fear rule; use a11y only when it genuinely degrades real usability, NEVER as "you
   could get sued". Minor SSL / cookie items stay in the msg-2 secondary slot.

Record the competing signals and why the lead won in `angle_rationale` (this is a
required reasoning field — name what it beat).

## A GENUINE SECONDARY POINT (use when the bundle has `secondary_signals`)

The bundle may include a `secondary_signals` object — concrete, OBJECTIVE facts beyond the
design/perf/content angle (advertising cookies before consent, AI-invisibility, mixed content,
SSL expiry). These are black-and-white and often legal/technical, so they land very differently
from a design opinion: they prove you actually looked closely, and they're hard to argue with.

**This point belongs in MESSAGE 2, not message 1.** It's the thing that gives the follow-up real
weight ("here's a relevant case study AND a concrete thing I found on your site"), so message 2
isn't just a link. Message 1 stays focused on the main angle. See the SECOND MESSAGE section.

Rules:
- Use **ONE** only — never stack two. If nothing's present, skip it (message 2 is just the case
  study / studio note, as before).
- Plain language, no jargon, framed as "worth a quick look", never fear-mongering. A genuine
  finding, not a second pitch.
- **When several are present, PREFER the rarer/more distinctive one** so messages vary:
  `ai_invisible` > `blocks_ai_crawlers` > `mixed_content` > `ssl` > `ad_cookies_before_consent`.
- Record it in `secondary_point` and the why in `secondary_reasoning` (or "none").

Phrasing guides (adapt, don't copy):
- **ad_cookies_before_consent** — this is a genuine COMPLIANCE matter, so frame it **seriously**
  (not a casual "worth a quick look" aside), while staying constructive (end on "quick fix"). Name
  the AD PRODUCTS, say the COOKIES are "set" (NEVER "fires", NEVER "GTM"). **Mention GDPR explicitly**,
  and phrase the EU angle as an **explicit assumption, never a stated fact** — we audit from a US IP
  and do NOT measure their EU traffic, so we must say **"i'm assuming you'll have eu visitors"**, never
  "you'll have eu visitors" or "you clearly get eu traffic". Ad cookies set before consent is a real
  GDPR / ePrivacy compliance matter for EU visitors.
  Write it like a real person texting a heads-up, NOT a compliance bot, and frame it as a **neutral
  compliance fact — NOT a threat.** **Banned (negative/fear) phrasing:** "the eu fines for", "the kind
  that gets enforced", "a genuine gdpr exposure", "not just a nitpick", "penalties", "at risk". Also
  **banned (asserting EU traffic as fact):** "you'll have eu visitors", "you clearly get eu traffic".
  Just say it needs to be GDPR-compliant, with the EU-traffic framed as an assumption:
  - `has_consent_banner` true (banner's there but not blocking): "also, quick heads up, your cookie
    banner shows but the {products} cookies are already set before anyone clicks accept, so it's not
    really gating them yet. i'm assuming you'll have eu visitors at your traffic, so that part just
    needs to be gdpr compliant. quick to sort though."
  - `has_consent_banner` false: "also, worth flagging, you've got {products} cookies loading the second
    someone lands, before any consent. i'm assuming you'll have eu visitors at your traffic, so worth
    getting that side gdpr compliant. not a big job."
- **ai_invisible_client_rendered**: "also, one separate thing, the site renders entirely in the
  browser, so chatgpt and google's ai overviews basically can't read you right now, which only
  matters more for getting found."
- **blocks_ai_crawlers**: "also worth checking, your robots setup is blocking the ai crawlers, so
  tools like chatgpt can't see the site."
- **mixed_content / ssl_expires_in_days**: cite plainly ("a few assets still load over http, which
  trips the browser's 'not secure' flag" / "your ssl cert looks set to expire in about {n} days").

Why cookies are handled this way: the bundle ONLY lists ad cookies that are genuinely written
before consent (cross-site identifiers like Meta Pixel / Google Ads — the serious, defensible
category). A harmless Consent-Mode cookieless ping stores nothing and is already excluded, and
analytics-only (`_ga` alone) is deliberately not raised. So you can state it confidently — just
keep the wording precise (cookies "set", real products, no "GTM").

## VOICE & FORMATTING

- Casual, human DM feel, like a real person typed it. No corporate polish. But use
  **proper capitalization and grammar**: capital at the start of every sentence, capital
  "I", real proper nouns/brands/acronyms cased correctly (Google, Webflow, GDPR, SEO, AI).
  Casual in tone, correct in mechanics. (A brand that is intentionally lowercase, e.g.
  "scite", "tapouts", keeps its own styling.)
- One idea per line, whitespace between lines. Skimmable in seconds.
- Plain words. Calm confidence. No hype.
- First message ~70-110 words. Closing ~25-45 words.

## HARD RULES (non-negotiable)

- **PROPER CAPITALIZATION IS MANDATORY — this OVERRIDES the casual feel.** Every message
  must use standard sentence case: a capital letter at the START of every sentence, the
  pronoun **"I" always capitalized** (I, I'd, I'm, I've), and real brands / proper nouns /
  acronyms cased correctly (Google, Webflow, GDPR, SEO, AI, ChatGPT). **Do NOT write in
  all-lowercase "texting" style.** Casual TONE, correct MECHANICS — they are not in conflict.
  ⚠️ The example message snippets elsewhere in this prompt are shown in lowercase to
  illustrate WORDING ONLY — you must still render every actual message in proper case.
  (The only exception is a brand that is intentionally lowercase, e.g. "scite", "tapouts".)
- **Address them only as the literal token `{first-name}`.** Never invent a name.
- **Never invent** numbers, funding, customers, percentages, or facts. Only what's
  in the bundle. Use exact figures/names as given.
- **No judgment, surprise, or fear.** Naming the specific thing plainly and kindly
  is required and is NOT criticism; what's banned is judging it (generic, letdown,
  dated, weak), acting surprised ("which is why i was surprised", "one thing that
  jumped out"), or loss framing ("costing you", "losing", "a drag on", "blocking",
  "missing out"). Frame every point as a gain.
- **Always end forward and positive.**
- No em dash, en dash, or double hyphen. Commas or periods only.
- No emojis, no exclamation marks.
- No jargon (LCP, INP, CLS, WCAG, contrast, headers, render). Plain words only.
- **Performance wording must match the metric:** load = slow to load / a faster
  site; responsiveness = laggy when you tap / responds instantly; layout shift =
  jumps around as it loads / stays steady. Never call lag or shift "slow loading".
- **Only use traffic/scale framing if `traffic_is_high` is true.** If false, make
  the outcome concrete another way (winning more of the buyers who already land,
  standing out to the exact audience they chase).
- **Accessibility:** only use it if genuinely severe (real critical/serious counts
  or lawsuit risk). If `a11y_not_measured`, don't use it.
- No buzzwords (cutting-edge, best-in-class, world-class, seamless, innovative,
  leverage, game-changing). No defensive hedging ("no pressure", "just trying to
  help", "sorry to bother").

## THE SEQUENCE (3 messages)

Each prospect gets a 3-touch sequence:
1. **first_message** — the inference-led DM (everything above).
2. **second_message** — a case-study follow-up: share the ONE most relevant case
   study, sent if they don't reply to the first.
3. **third_message** — a very short, low-key close, sent if still no reply.

## TECH STACK & STUDIO IDENTITY (when and how we introduce ourselves)

Entries are **sorted by `tech_stack`** (the `dump` step groups them). How we introduce who
we are depends on whether the prospect is ALREADY on Webflow:

- **Webflow prospects** (`tech_stack` == webflow): in the **FIRST message**, naturally mention
  we are a **"webflow certified partner"** — their being on Webflow is exactly why it lands as a
  credible reason to reach out. Do this ONLY for Webflow sites. Their SECOND message then does
  NOT re-introduce us.
- **Every other stack** (next.js, react, framer, custom/static, wordpress, etc.): the FIRST
  message makes **no mention of who we are**. Instead, the **SECOND message** introduces us, and
  NOT as a "webflow agency" but as **"a design and webflow studio"** (softer — an identity, not
  a hard pitch).

## SECOND MESSAGE (case study, conversational)

A short, genuine continuation — NOT a sales follow-up. **Never use the words "following up"**
(or anything that reads like a sales cadence). It should feel like a person sharing something
relevant they thought of.

### RELEVANCE GATE (decide this FIRST)
Only include a case study **link** if it is **strongly relevant** to THIS prospect —
same/adjacent industry, OR the core problem+solution genuinely transfers (e.g. a performance fix
for a real speed problem, an AI-product rebuild for an AI product, a Webflow perf fix for a
Webflow site, a premium-product launch site for a premium product). Do NOT force a weak case
study. (Bad example: Minerva, an education *program*, paired with Qmin, an insurance-commission
SaaS — different worlds, the solution wouldn't transfer.)

**If nothing is strongly relevant, STILL write message 2 — just WITHOUT a case study.** Never
leave the second touch blank. Write a genuine second note that builds on message 1's point,
introduces who we are (identity rules below), and ends with a soft offer ("happy to show you how
we'd approach {them}"). Set `case_study_name`/`case_study_url` to "none". A case-study-less
message 2 is the fallback, not a missing message.

### When a case study IS strongly relevant, shape:
- **The opener must STAND ALONE** — message 2 arrives later; assume they don't remember message 1.
  NEVER open with "this one's close to your space/world" (it references a message they've forgotten,
  AND it falsely claims industry closeness). Instead introduce the client work: "we recently worked
  with {company}, {what they are}, on a similar challenge to yours." For non-Webflow prospects, lead
  with the identity first ("hey {first-name}, quick context, we're a design and webflow studio. we
  recently worked with…"); for Webflow (already introduced in msg 1), skip straight to the client.
- **Never claim industry closeness ("your space") unless the industries actually match.** The case
  study was often picked for a shared PROBLEM, not industry. State the client's REAL industry
  honestly, and frame the link as "a similar challenge/problem to yours" — never imply same space.
- the case-study link on its own line: `https://prismport.co/case-studies/<slug>`
- one line stating what we did for that company + its **real** result, as PROOF of what we can do:
  "we rebuilt {company} and {exact result}".
- **NEVER promise the prospect that same result.** Do NOT write "the same lift we'd bring to you",
  "exactly the result we'd get you", etc. — we cannot claim we'd reproduce a specific metric. End
  instead with an honest relevance tie ("felt close to what you're working through with {their
  point}") and/or a confident OFFER ("happy to show you how we'd approach yours"). Confident, not
  a hedging question, and never an outcome promise.

### THEN, if the bundle has a secondary signal, append the concrete finding
After the case-study (or studio note), add the ONE secondary point as a final short line (see
"A GENUINE SECONDARY POINT" above for phrasing). This is what gives message 2 weight — it shows
you actually audited their site, not just dropped a link. It reads as "…and one concrete thing I
spotted while I was in there." Keep the case study / offer as the body; the finding is the closer.
If there's no secondary signal, message 2 just ends on the offer, as before.

**Keep it tight: ~40-70 words** (the secondary finding adds a line). Same hard rules as message 1
(proper capitalization, no dash, no buzzwords, `{first-name}`, never invent numbers). Use the case study's
real metric exactly as written.

## THIRD MESSAGE (soft goodbye)

Very short and genuinely low-key: a graceful goodbye, not a pitch. **Do NOT say "totally
fair"** (it implies their not-replying is fine/expected). **Do NOT use the tired "now or later /
now-or-later kind of thing" construction** (it's clunky and was overused). Proper capitalization.
**Use ONLY these four approved closes** (rotate across prospects; do not invent new ones):
- "Hey {first-name}, I'll leave this with you for now. Whenever it feels like the right time, I'd
  be glad to talk through how we'd approach it."
- "Hey {first-name}, no rush on this at all. If it ever moves up your list, I'm happy to jump on a
  quick call and show you what we'd do."
- "Hey {first-name}, I'll stop here so I'm not crowding your inbox. Whenever the timing's right for
  a quick chat, I'm around and happy to help."
- "Hey {first-name}, totally understand if this isn't top of mind right now. It'll keep, so reach
  out whenever you'd like to dig into it."

## OUTPUT SCHEMA (write to data/message_results.json)

A JSON array. One object per prospect:

```json
[
  {
    "domain": "<exact domain from the bundle>",
    "priority": "high|medium|low|skip",
    "signal_category": "performance|design|content|accessibility|other",
    "chosen_signal": "<the one area, short>",
    "angle_rationale": "<WHY this angle over the other signals in the audit: name the competing signals (scores, perf ratings, secondary flags) and say why this one is the honest, strongest, most opportunity-framed lead. 1-2 sentences. This reasoning drives message quality, so make it real.>",
    "inference": "<the business inference you led with, one line>",
    "why_it_matters": "<the concrete business outcome, with scale math if traffic_is_high>",
    "use_traffic_scale": true,
    "genuine_positive": "<the real strength you credited, or 'none'>",
    "quotable_fact_to_use": "<exact fact woven in, or 'none'>",
    "secondary_point": "<the one secondary signal you raised in MESSAGE 2, e.g. 'ad cookies before consent: Meta Pixel, Google Ads', or 'none'>",
    "secondary_reasoning": "<why you chose it (and over what), like a reason line — or 'none'>",
    "case_study_name": "<which case study you chose for message 2>",
    "case_study_rationale": "<WHY this case study is the honest best fit: problem-match first, then industry adjacency; note if it's a stretch and how you kept the relevance line honest. 1-2 sentences.>",
    "case_study_url": "<https://prismport.co/case-studies/<slug>>",
    "first_message": "<the inference DM>",
    "second_message": "<the case-study follow-up>",
    "third_message": "<the short soft close>"
  }
]
```

If a prospect is genuinely not worth messaging, set `priority` to `skip` and leave
the messages empty.

**EVERY field above is REQUIRED for every non-skip entry** — not just the three messages.
`chosen_signal`, `inference`, `why_it_matters`, and `genuine_positive` are easy to forget if you
build the JSON with a compact helper, but they must be filled (use `"none"` only where the schema
explicitly allows it). `prep_bundles.py assemble` **validates this and will refuse to write the CSV**
if any non-skip entry is missing a required field (listing exactly what's missing). Do not bypass it
with `--allow-incomplete` unless you truly mean to. If you generate in batches, give each batch the
full field set — never a shortcut that drops the analyst metadata.
