### **Your Culture — turning something invisible into something unforgettable**

# Your Culture had submitted a partner request on Webflow looking for help with their website. We reached out, hopped on a call, and met Josh, Joe, and Scott, the co founders. The existing site was just a one pager. A form. A few logos. One line that said “your digital street team.” That was it. Even after looking through it beforehand, the business itself wasn’t clear.

# Within the first few minutes, it was obvious that this was not like a typical SaaS product. Your Culture works with artists, labels, and brands by activating real fans at scale. They manage communities that create content, spread hype, and drive engagement around releases, tours, and moments. It’s the kind of work that looks organic from the outside. You see a reel blow up, a trend catch fire, a concert suddenly everywhere. You don’t see the system behind it.

# Hearing that, it clicked. There really was a business here. A serious one. And most people would never realise it existed.

# That made the project interesting.

# The idea was sharp, the industry was creative, and the problem was clear. Your Culture didn’t need a website to generate leads. Most of their work already came through relationships and reputation. What they lacked was a place that explained who they are, what they do, and why it matters, both for brands and for fans.

# Budget wise, it was modest. They didn’t expect the site to drive revenue directly, so they hadn’t planned for a big build. We talked through that honestly. We also pointed out something they hadn’t fully considered yet. Their audience wasn’t just labels. Fans were landing on the site too. And right now, there was nothing there that explained why someone should join this community or what being part of it actually looks like.

# That part landed.

# Once we aligned on that, the direction became much clearer.

# We started with words, not visuals. The site needed two paths from the very beginning. One for brands and labels evaluating a partner. One for fans trying to understand what they’re joining. That split shaped everything that followed. Content came first. Structure followed. Only then did we move into design.

# Creatively, this project pushed us far outside our usual comfort zone, and that was intentional. The brands Your Culture works with live in a very expressive, visual world. Music labels don’t want sterile websites. They want energy. Confidence. Something that feels alive.

# Your Culture already had a strong colour at the centre of their brand. That hot pink became the anchor. We leaned into it fully. The layouts went bold. Typography became expressive. Spacing broke rules on purpose. We explored interactions early and often, not as decoration, but as part of how the site communicates.

# Motion played a huge role. GSAP powered transitions, interactive cards, horizontal scroll sections, and subtle parallax across pages. The site moves because the brand moves. It doesn’t sit still.

# Even with all of that, performance mattered. The experience had to feel smooth on mobile and desktop. Our developers spent serious time tuning execution, being selective about where motion lived and how it loaded. In the end, a site full of animation shipped with a Lighthouse performance score of 97, and perfect scores across accessibility, best practices, and SEO. That wasn’t accidental. It was a choice.

# Today, Your Culture has a place that actually reflects their energy. A site that feels as bold as the work they do behind the scenes. A clear explanation for brands. A compelling invitation for fans.

# And for us, it stands as one of those projects you point to and say, this is what happens when you let the idea lead and trust the process.
