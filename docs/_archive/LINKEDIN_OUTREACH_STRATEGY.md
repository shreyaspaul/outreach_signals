# LinkedIn Outreach Strategy: WordPress → Webflow Migration
## Data-Driven Messaging for $10-20K Deals

---

## Executive Summary

Based on analysis of 316 fully-enriched prospects, we've identified **4 high-value segments** with distinct pain points and messaging angles. The data reveals that strict signal adherence (Funded + WordPress, etc.) is too narrow - only 27 WordPress companies total, with just 6 having performance issues.

**The opportunity**: Expand beyond WordPress to target ANY platform with measurable quality issues. The real signal isn't the CMS - it's the gap between their growth stage and their website's ability to support it.

## LinkedIn Message Framework

### **Connection Request Formula** (300 char max)
```
[Observation about their company] + [Reason for connection] + [Low-friction close]
```

**Avoid**:
- "I help companies..." (immediately salesy)
- "I was impressed by..." (generic flattery)
- "I'd love to connect..." (why should they care?)

**Do**:
- Lead with insight about THEM
- Reference something specific (funding, product, team size)
- Make it feel like peer-to-peer, not vendor-to-prospect

---

### **First Message Formula** (After Connection Accepts)
```
[Specific observation with data] + [Business implication] + [Soft question]
```

**Structure**:
1. **Line 1**: The insight (specific, data-backed)
2. **Line 2**: Why it matters to their business
3. **Line 3**: Low-pressure question or offer

**Timing**: Send 2-4 hours after connection accepts (not instant, not days later)

## Message Templates by Segment

### **SEGMENT 1: Performance Bleed**

#### Connection Request:
```
Saw you hit [X traffic milestone]. Running a quick audit on high-traffic SaaS sites - noticed your mobile load time might be costing conversions. Worth a look?
```

#### First Message (High Traffic + Slow Mobile):
```
Your site's getting 1M+ visits/month but loads in 8+ seconds on mobile. At that traffic level, every second of delay is likely costing you 5-10% of mobile conversions.

I run technical migrations for funded SaaS companies - we've recovered 20-40% conversion lifts just by rebuilding for speed. No pitch, but if you're curious what's causing the drag, I can send you a quick breakdown.

Worth 15 min?
```

**Why It Works**:
- Opens with their specific data (1M visits, 8 seconds)
- Quantifies the cost (5-10% conversions)
- Positions expertise without pitching (social proof: "funded SaaS companies")
- Offers value first (breakdown), ask second
- Soft close (15 min vs. "demo" or "call")

---

#### Alternative: High Traffic + High Bounce:
```
Pulled your site metrics - 300K visits/month but 79% bounce rate. That's not a product problem, that's a first-impression problem.

Usually caused by slow load times or unclear value prop above the fold. We rebuilt [similar company]'s site and dropped their bounce from 75% to 42% in 6 weeks.

Mind if I send over what I'd look at first?
```

**Why It Works**:
- Separates product quality from site quality (ego protection)
- Specific comparison (75% → 42%)
- Asks permission to help (consultative, not pushy)

## Sequencing Strategy

### **Timing Between Touches**

| Touch | Channel | Timing | Goal |
|-------|---------|--------|------|
| Touch 1 | LinkedIn Connection | Day 0 | Get accepted |
| Touch 2 | LinkedIn Message | Day 0 (2-4hrs after accept) | Spark interest |
| Touch 3 | Email (if have it) | Day 3 | Different channel, more detail |
| Touch 4 | LinkedIn | Day 7 | New angle or case study |
| Touch 5 | Email + LinkedIn | Day 14 | Break-up or final value |

### **Multi-Channel Approach**

**LinkedIn + Email = 2-3x response rate** when done right.

**Rules**:
1. **Different content** for each channel (don't copy-paste)
2. **Email goes deeper** (can be 2-3x longer, include links/attachments)
3. **LinkedIn is conversational** (short, punchy, peer-to-peer)
4. **Reference the other channel** sparingly ("Sent you a note on email with specifics")

## Advanced Tactics

### **1. The Competitive Trigger**

When you see 2+ companies in the same space, message the lower-performing one:

```
I was doing site audits for [competitor] and noticed you're both in the [space] but their site loads 2x faster. Figured you'd want to know since you're both fighting for the same buyers.
```

**Why It Works**: Fear of losing to competitors > desire to improve in isolation

---

### **2. The Hiring Signal**

If they're hiring marketers/growth roles:

```
Saw you're hiring a Growth Lead. Smart move - but your site's current performance (mobile 37) is going to frustrate whoever you hire. They'll spend their first 3 months fighting the CMS instead of running experiments.
```

**Why It Works**: Reframes site quality as an employee retention/productivity issue

---

### **3. The Funding Announcement**

Within 2 weeks of funding announcement:

```
Congrats on the Series A. First 90 days post-funding is when you'll get the most inbound interest from prospects checking you out. Your site's content score is 12/100 right now - that's a terrible first impression for the spike in traffic you're about to get.
```

**Why It Works**: Time-sensitive (90-day window), ties to immediate business event

---

### **4. The Post-Event**

After they speak at a conference or get press:

```
Saw your TechCrunch feature - great coverage. Checked your site traffic and you spiked 10x yesterday. Unfortunately your mobile PageSpeed is 28, so most of that mobile traffic probably bounced before the page loaded.
```

**Why It Works**: Ties to real-time event they care about, quantifiable missed opportunity

## Response Handling

### **When They Reply: "Not a priority right now"**

**Response**:
```
Totally get it - site infrastructure is never urgent until it is.

Just so it's on your radar: at 200K visits/month, a 1-second speed improvement typically = 7-10% conversion lift. For most SaaS companies that's 6-figure ARR impact annually.

When it does become a priority, we can move fast (4-6 weeks). I'll check back in Q2.
```

**Why It Works**:
- Accepts their answer (no pressure)
- Plants the seed with data (6-figure ARR impact)
- Sets expectation for future touchpoint (Q2)

---

### **When They Reply: "Send me pricing"**

**Response**:
```
Happy to, but pricing varies based on scope (# of pages, integrations, custom functionality).

30-min call to understand your setup and I can send a fixed-price proposal same day. Usually lands between $12-18K for companies at your stage.

[Calendar link] work?
```

**Why It Works**:
- Doesn't dodge the question (gives range)
- Explains why you need the call (logical, not evasive)
- Fast turnaround (same day proposal)
- Sets expectations ($12-18K range)

---

### **When They Reply: "We're on [other platform], not interested in migrating"**

**Response**:
```
Fair - but the issue isn't [platform], it's performance. You could stay on [platform] and fix the speed issues, or migrate to something built for it. Either way, you're bleeding conversions right now.

If you want to optimize [platform] first, I can point you to specialists. If you want to see what a proper migration looks like, I'm here. Either way, you should fix this.
```

**Why It Works**:
- Reframes (it's not about the platform)
- Offers alternative (optimize vs. migrate)
- Helpful even if they don't buy from you
- Urgency (bleeding conversions)

## Do's and Don'ts

### **DO**:
✓ Use their specific data (traffic, PageSpeed scores, bounce rate)
✓ Lead with business impact, not technical features
✓ Reference their funding/growth stage
✓ Sound like a peer, not a vendor
✓ Offer value before asking for time
✓ Keep LinkedIn messages <100 words
✓ Make it easy to say yes (small asks, soft CTAs)
✓ Follow up consistently (7-14 day gaps)

### **DON'T**:
✗ Trash their current platform (WordPress, etc.)
✗ Use buzzwords ("best-in-class," "cutting-edge")
✗ Send generic templates
✗ Ask for 30-60 min calls upfront (too much friction)
✗ Pitch features (Webflow's CMS, visual editor, etc.)
✗ Follow up more than 3-4 times
✗ Sound desperate ("just checking in")
✗ Use emojis or exclamation points

Test monthly with 50/50 splits:

1. **Subject line length** (3 words vs. 6 words)
2. **Data-heavy vs. story-heavy** (metrics vs. case study)
3. **Ask timing** (value first vs. meeting first)
4. **Social proof type** (company names vs. metrics)
5. **Message length** (50 words vs. 100 words)

## Final Thoughts

The data shows our best opportunities are NOT the strict WordPress signals we initially targeted. Instead:

1. **Prioritize performance issues** on ANY platform - it's measurable pain with clear ROI
2. **Funded companies with quality gaps** are everywhere - 198 prospects vs. 27 WordPress-only
3. **Platform is secondary** to business impact (conversions, credibility, velocity)
4. **Message specificity** matters more than volume - use the actual data you have

The key is sounding like you actually looked at their site, understand their business stage, and can quantify the gap between where they are and where they should be.

Less "we're a Webflow agency" and more "you're leaving money on the table, here's how much, here's how to fix it."
